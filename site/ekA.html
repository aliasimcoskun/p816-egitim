A.1A–B: Air Boss … Buster

Bu ek, T-45C için gemi (CV) prosedürlerinde kullanılan terimlerin sözlüğüdür (A100. Glossary of CV Procedures for T-45C). Terimler aşağıda İngilizce başlıklarıyla, alfabetik gruplar hâlinde verilmiştir.

    Tanımı görmek için listeden bir terim seçin.

    Tanımlar bu sayfadaki tablolarla aynıdır (Ek A, s. A-1 – A-6). Arama büyük/küçük harf ve Türkçe karakter (ı/i, ş/s, ğ/g, ü/u, ö/o, ç/c) farkı gözetmez. ◆ işaretli terimlerde belgede bir tutarsızlık vardır; ayrıntı derste mor Kaynak notu kutusundadır.

    1. A-1.02Air Boss: Officer (located in Pri-Fly) in charge of all flight deck and tower operations within 5 nautical miles of the ship
    2. A-1.03Air Operations Officer: The officer who coordinates all matters pertaining to air operations including CATCC
    3. A-1.04Air Plan: Schedule of carrier flight operations published daily but subject to change
    4. A-1.05Angels: Altitude in thousands of feet. For example, Angels 1.5 = 1,500 feet
    5. A-1.06Axial Winds: Winds down the longitudinal axis of the ship created by the ship’s forward movement.
    6. A-1.07This causes a right-to-left crosswind across the angled deck.
    7. A-1.08Ball: See “Meatball.”
    8. A-1.09Bingo: Refers to the minimum fuel state required to divert safely to the nearest suitable field.
    9. A-1.10Bingo is an emergency situation.
    10. A-1.11Bingo Fuel: Aircraft fuel state in sufficient quantity necessary to fly to the bingo field with X lbs. remaining; depending on aircraft type.
    11. A-1.12Bolter: A touchdown on the carrier in which the arresting hook does not engage the arresting wires
    12. A-1.13BRC: Base recovery course, which is the ship’s magnetic course
    13. A-1.14Breaking the Deck: First aircraft to land for each cycle
    14. A-1.15Buster: Proceed at maximum airspeed, generally for an immediate Charlie
    15. A-1.16Carrier Air Traffic Control Center (CATCC): The centralized department responsible for the status-keeping of all carrier air operations and control of all airborne aircraft involved in launch and recovery
    16. A-1.17Carrier Control Zone (CCZ): The airspace within a circular limit defined by a 5 mile radius around the ship surface up to and including 2,500 feet under the cognizance of the Air Boss during VFR conditions
    17. A-2.01Case I: Refers to departure/recovery procedures and landing patterns conducted in VMC conditions when 3,000/5 or greater exist (3,000-foot ceiling and 5-NM visibility within the carrier control zone).
    18. A-2.02Case I recoveries will marshal overhead the ship and enter the pattern via the break.
    19. A-2.03Case II: Weather less than 3,000/5 but greater than 1,000/5 exist at the ship.
    20. A-2.04Case II recovery is a controlled IMC descent to the break and the VFR pattern.
    21. A-2.05It is used when a VFR penetration cannot be made.
    22. A-2.06The approach may be via radar vectors or a TACAN or ADF approach.
    23. A-2.07In no case will more than a section of two aircraft execute a Case II recovery.
    24. A-2.08Case II departure is a procedure used to climb through IFR conditions to VMC.
    25. A-2.09Case III: Used for weather less than 1,000/5, or at night.
    26. A-2.10CCA: Carrier-controlled approach similar to a GCA
    27. A-2.11Charlie: Refers to the time the first aircraft is expected at the ramp.
    28. A-2.12A “Charlie” or “Charlie on arrival” call is a directive to enter the pattern now.
    29. A-2.13“Charlie five” means be at the ramp in five minutes.
    30. A-2.14Cherubs: Altitude in hundreds of feet. For example, Cherubs 3 = 300 feet (Normally used for helicopters).
    31. A-2.15Chicks: Wingmen in a flight
    32. A-2.16Clara: Meatball is not in sight
    33. A-2.17Clearing Turn: Associated with a Case I or II departure.
    34. A-2.18Immediately after launch, aircraft from bow cats initiate a right turn then a turn to parallel the BRC.
    35. A-2.19Aircraft launched from the waist cats initiate a left turn then a turn to parallel the BRC.
    36. A-2.20The purpose of these turns is to provide aircraft lateral separation on multiple launches from the carrier.
    37. A-2.21C.Q.: Carrier qualification
    38. A-2.22Cross-Deck Pendant (CDP): Arresting gear wire
    39. A-2.23Cut Lights: Green lights mounted horizontally and centered above the IFLOLS lens box (controlled by the LSO).
    40. A-2.24Utilized during Zip Lip and EMCON conditions instead of UHF to give pilots clearance to land, i.e., “Roger Ball.
    41. A-2.25Subsequent cut lights mean “power.
    42. A-2.26Also, used in conjunction with waveoff lights to signal bingo.
    43. A-2.27Datum Lights: Green reference lights mounted horizontally on the IFLOLS, seen on each side of the centered cell
    44. A-2.28Delta: Enter holding pattern as directed or continue to hold
    45. A-3.01Delta Clean: Signal for aircraft in the pattern to raise gear and flaps/slats and hold as directed
    46. A-3.02Delta Easy: Signal for aircraft to remain at pattern altitude with gear and flaps/slats down and speed brakes retracted
    47. A-3.03Departure Reference Radial (DRR): Preassigned radial usually passed during the brief or as standing SOP
    48. A-3.04Divert: An order for an aircraft to proceed and land at the field specified.
    49. A-3.05This is a non- emergency situation.
    50. A-3.06Emergency Expected Approach Time (EEAT): The future time, assigned prior to launch, at which time an aircraft is cleared to depart inbound or penetrate from a pre-assigned fix under lost communications conditions
    51. A-3.07Emergency Marshal: A marshal established by CATCC and given to each pilot prior to launch with and altitude and EEAT.
    52. A-3.08The emergency marshal radial will have a minimum of 30 degrees of separation from the primary marshal radial.
    53. A-3.09Emission Control Procedures (EMCON): Electronic emission control procedures are in effect at the ship to avoid detection.
    54. A-3.10All radio, radar, and navigation equipment transmissions are eliminated except as required for safety of flight.
    55. A-3.11Expected Approach Time (EAT): The future time at which an aircraft is cleared to depart inbound or penetrate from a pre-assigned fix.
    56. A-3.12Aircraft depart and commence approach at assigned time if no further instructions are received.
    57. A-3.13Father: Code name for the ship’s TACAN
    58. A-3.14Feet Wet or Feet Dry: Aircraft crossing the coastline enroute to or returning from the ship
    59. A-3.15Field Carrier Landing Practice (FCLP): LSO-graded landings conducted at the field prior to any carrier evolution
    60. A-3.16Final Bearing (FB): The magnetic bearing assigned by CATCC for final approach (an extension of the landing area centerline); usually BRC minus the landing area angle of 10°.
    61. A-3.17Foul Deck: Landing area is not free of all obstructions or the flight deck is not ready to recover aircraft.
    62. A-3.18Foul Line (ship only): A line painted on both sides of the landing area to define the minimum area that must be free of obstructions in order to consider the deck clear
    63. A-3.19Fuel Ladder: A quick snapshot reference of total fuel breakdown and airborne time remaining used in the formulation of fuel requirement decision making
    64. A-4.01Gadget: Radar
    65. A-4.02Hangar Deck: Area below the flight deck used to store and repair aircraft
    66. A-4.03Hawk: Term used for a lead safe. (Hawking chicks)
    67. A-4.04Holdback: Metal fitting designed to break or release at a preset level of force during a catapult stroke
    68. A-4.05Hold-Down: Fuel state at which an aircraft will be refueled on deck prior to launch
    69. A-4.06Hook to Eye: The vertical distance measured between the pilot’s eye and the aircraft’s hook
    70. A-4.07Hook to Ramp: The clearance distance between the aircraft’s hook point and the flight deck as it crosses the ramp
    71. A-4.08Hot Refueling: Aircraft receives fuel with engine turning
    72. A-4.09Hot Seat: The replacement of one pilot by another pilot while the engine is turning
    73. A-4.10Improved Fresnel Lens Optical Landing System (IFLOLS): Pilot’s landing aid, i.e., meatball
    74. A-4.11Interval: The time between you and the aircraft you are to follow
    75. A-4.12In the Middle Position: A distance on the groove that is between the “start” and the “in close” position.
    76. A-4.13The middle-third of the groove.
    77. A-4.14Jet Blast Deflector (JBD): Hydraulically lifted deck plate mounted behind each catapult
    78. A-4.15Kilo Report: An aircrew coded report indicating aircraft mission readiness
    79. A-4.16Landing Signals Officer (LSO). Controls all fixed-wing aircraft off the 180 to touchdown during carrier and FCLP landings
    80. A-4.17Launch Bar: Metal arm attached to the nose gear and used to launch the aircraft
    81. A-4.18Mark your Father: State bearing and distance from ship
    82. A-4.19Marshal:
    83. A-4.20(1) Holding pattern during Case I, II, and III recoveries
    84. A-4.21(2) The term used for the ship’s radar controller
    85. A-4.22Meatball: Light projected by source lens on the IFLOLS
    86. A-5.01Mirror: Landing aid used prior to the development of the Fresnel lens
    87. A-5.02Mother: Code name used to signify the carrier
    88. A-5.03Ninety-nine Aircraft: A collective call to all aircraft in the launch or recovery
    89. A-5.04On the Ball: LSO call stating hold your transmission until aircraft in the groove has landed
    90. A-5.05Overhead Time: The scheduled time a flight of aircraft is expected overhead the ship for pattern entry
    91. A-5.06Paddles: The call sign for the LSO
    92. A-5.07Parrot: IFF
    93. A-5.08Pigeons: The magnetic bearing and distance to the divert field named
    94. A-5.09Pilot Landing Assistance Television (PLAT): Video camera system used to record carrier operations
    95. A-5.10PIM: Position of intended movement
    96. A-5.11Plane Guard: SAR helicopter or ship assigned during aircraft launch and recovery, usually located in starboard Delta for a helicopter, three miles astern for a ship
    97. A-5.12Platform: A reporting point in the ship’s TACAN approach (normally at 20 NM from the ship at 5,000 feet) at which the rate of descent is decreased to 2,000 feet per minute
    98. A-5.13Pogo: Return to previous frequency if unable to establish communications on frequency assigned
    99. A-5.14Popeye: Code word used to signify that aircraft is operating IMC
    100. A-5.15Pri-Fly: Tower location where the Air Boss oversees the pattern and flight deck operations
    101. A-5.16Primary Marshal Radial: The radial assigned by Marshal that is used as the primary if there is a requirement for more than one Marshal stack/radial (geopolitical constraints or additional battle groups operating in the same area)
    102. A-5.17Pull Back: Action following arrestment whereby the wire is partially retracted to allow the pilot to raise the tailhook
    103. A-5.18Push Back: Action taken anytime the aircraft needs to be moved back by deck personnel
    104. A-5.19Ramp: The aft end of the flight deck.
    105. A-6.01Ramp Time (Ready Deck): Anticipated time specified by the Air Boss that the flight deck will be ready to recover aircraft.
    106. A-6.02Time the first aircraft in Case III recovery is expected to be at ramp.
    107. A-6.03Red Crown: Air Defense Unit generally located on a Destroyer (DDG) or CG that protects the battle group airspace, and verifies IFF checks
    108. A-6.04Roger Ball: The call made by the LSO that indicates you are cleared to land and the LSO has positive control (call made less than a mile prior to landing)
    109. A-6.05Roll Angle: Movement of the lens about the roll axis (set for each type of aircraft) to maintain a constant targeted hook touchdown point
    110. A-6.06Round Down: The aft end of the landing area that is curved downward
    111. A-6.07RTB: Signal to return to base
    112. A-6.08See You: Communication used to indicate that flight lead has the ship in sight
    113. A-6.09Shuttle: The portion of the catapult that attaches to the launch bar during catapult launches
    114. A-6.10Spin: A delaying circle at 1200’, performed at the bow when the pattern is too full to allow all members of the flight to break by 4 NM.
    115. A-6.11Starboard Delta: Holding pattern used by the helicopters and COD aircraft flown on the starboard side of the ship and using right-hand turns at 500 feet
    116. A-6.12Start: The first-third of the groove length
    117. A-6.13Steer: A heading to an airfield for normal divert from the ship when not in bingo profile.
    118. A-6.14When directed, proceed to the field named.
    119. A-6.15Strangle Your Parrot: Turn off your IFF.
    120. A-6.16Suspend: Stop the catapult launch sequence.
    121. A-6.17Sweet Lock: Positive TACAN lock-on
    122. A-6.18Tension: The portion of the catapult launch sequence when the shuttle is hydraulically moved forward to remove slack
    123. A-6.19Tiedown: Chocks and chains used to secure aircraft on the flight deck
    124. A-6.20Trick or Treat: Aircraft in pattern that has enough fuel for one more approach.
    125. A-6.21If the aircraft does not trap, it will have to bingo or in-flight refuel (if able).
    126. A-6.22Zip Lip: Condition in which radio communications are minimized

    Sözlüğe göre Air Boss, geminin ne kadar çevresindeki uçuş güvertesi ve kule operasyonlarından sorumludur?

    "Officer (located in Pri-Fly) in charge of all flight deck and tower operations within 5 nautical miles of the ship."

    Kaynak: s. A-1 (A-1.02)

    Telsizde "Angels 1.5" hangi irtifayı ifade eder?

    Angels = bin feet cinsinden irtifa; Angels 1.5 = 1.500 ft. (Yüz feet cinsinden irtifa "Cherubs"tır.)

    Kaynak: s. A-1 (A-1.05)

    Sözlüğe göre axial winds açılı güverte (angled deck) üzerinde nasıl bir yan rüzgâr oluşturur?

    Geminin ileri hareketinin oluşturduğu, boyuna eksen boyunca esen rüzgâr; "This causes a right-to-left crosswind across the angled deck."

    Kaynak: s. A-1 (A-1.06, A-1.07)

    Sözlüğe göre Bingo ve Divert için hangisi doğrudur?

    Bingo, en yakın uygun meydana emniyetle divert için gereken asgari yakıt durumudur ve "an emergency situation"dır. Divert, belirtilen meydana gidip inme emridir ve "a non-emergency situation"dır.

    Kaynak: s. A-1, A-3 (A-1.09, A-1.10, A-3.04, A-3.05)

    Bolter nedir?

    "A touchdown on the carrier in which the arresting hook does not engage the arresting wires."

    Kaynak: s. A-1 (A-1.12)

    Sözlüğe göre "Buster" ne demektir?

    "Proceed at maximum airspeed, generally for an immediate Charlie." (Kalkış sırasını durdurmak Suspend, önceki frekansa dönmek Pogo, beklemek Delta'dır.)

    Kaynak: s. A-1 (A-1.15)

Bölüm 2'de break'in 4 NM'den önce yapılamaması ayrı bir prosedüre bağlanır: flight paterni terk edip yeniden girer (5 NM'ye kadar 800 ft, sonra 1.200 ft ve sola arc; 2-15.17, 2-15.18).
